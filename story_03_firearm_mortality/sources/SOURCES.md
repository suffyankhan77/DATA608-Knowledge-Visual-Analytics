# Sources and reproducible methods

## Firearm mortality

- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, National Center for Health Statistics, *NCHS - VSRR Quarterly provisional estimates for selected indicators of mortality*. Dataset and API: https://data.cdc.gov/National-Center-for-Health-Statistics/NCHS-VSRR-Quarterly-provisional-estimates-for-sele/489q-934x and https://data.cdc.gov/resource/489q-934x.json .
- Query parameters: `year_and_quarter='2024 Q4'`, `time_period='12 months ending with quarter'`, `cause_of_death='Firearm-related injury'`, and `rate_type='Age-adjusted'`. Extract the 50 `rate_<state>` values from the one matching API record. This is the annual 12-month age-adjusted rate per 100,000 ending in 2024 Q4, not the three-month annualized rate and not the crude rate. The saved JSON includes the literal request URL, response, and retrieval time.
- CDC, *Firearm Mortality, Stats of the States*: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/state-stats/deaths/firearms.html . This page describes the 2024 final age-adjusted rates. Spot checks of the API record with the displayed CDC 2024 values for New York (4.4), Mississippi (28.0), California (7.0), and Massachusetts (3.8) match at the reported one-decimal precision. The API dataset is described as provisional and could later be revised. The assignment's legacy `/nchs/pressroom/sosmap/firearm_mortality/firearm.htm` link returned 404 on September 27, 2026.
- Firearm-related injury includes firearm deaths by suicide, homicide, and other intents. Avoid calling the all-intents rate "gun violence homicides." Rates count state of residence and adjust age distribution. They are not a direct measure of the effects of a law.

## Gun law strength

- GIFFORDS Law Center, *2024 Annual Gun Law Scorecard*: https://giffords.org/resources/scorecard2024/ . Saved HTML and extracted 50 state grades and strength ranks are included. GIFFORDS is an advocacy organization. Its grades encode its own policy selection and weighting; another index could give different classifications.
- Convert original grade A/A- to 5 (strictest), B+/B/B- to 4, C+/C/C- to 3, D+/D/D- to 2, and F to 1 (most lax). Preserve original grades and original strength rankings in the joined CSV. This gives five ordered bins for the assignment without inventing individual state classifications. The derived bin sizes are 13, 7, 5, 3, and 22 respectively from strictest to most lax; they are unequal.
- Do **not** use the death rates printed in the 2024 GIFFORDS table. Its mortality figures refer to an earlier release and are not the independently requested 2024 CDC API rates. `scripts/fetch_and_prepare.py` extracts only the law grade and strength rank.

## Computation and visual choices

- Join by exact state name and abbreviation; assert 50 unique states and no missing mortality or law levels. Exclude the District of Columbia because the scorecard grades the 50 states and the assignment asks for each state.
- Descriptive statistic: mean and median of state-level age-adjusted rates within each law category. These are unweighted summaries of the states and **not** a pooled national death rate or a population-weighted individual risk.
- Spearman correlation of ordinal law level with state mortality is **-0.717** (exploratory two-sided p approximately `4.72e-9`). A small p value for this observational ecological comparison does not eliminate confounding, make the states independent, or identify a policy effect. Visual conclusions focus on group differences and exceptions.
- The two primary heat maps show one tile per state at schematic positions; the tile areas are equal and do not depict physical state area, distance, or precise adjacencies. The state maps now include direct takeaways, printed law levels, and numbered outlines linking Hawaii, Mississippi and New Mexico to their annotations. The mortality-band heat map prints state counts and within-group percentages, with color encoding the percentage on a fixed 0-to-100 scale. Black outlines emphasize the F group at 16 or more deaths (15/22, 68%) and the A group below 8 (7/13, 54%). This makes the message visible on the chart and accounts for unequal group sizes. Rounded cell percentages may not sum to 100%. The base R `hcl.colors(..., 'Viridis')` sequential and five-level palettes make labels readable and distinguish values without relying on red/green opposition. The category map and mortality map have separate legends and scales.
- The 2024 scorecard may include laws enacted during the year. The 2024 death rates occur in the same period. Gun ownership, demographics, economic conditions, geography, enforcement, neighboring laws, and policy timing could influence differences. State ecological comparisons cannot show that stricter laws **result in** fewer deaths. The supported answer is an observed inverse association consistent with, but not proof of, a beneficial effect.
